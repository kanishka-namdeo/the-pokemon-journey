# Scrollytelling topic research — candidate tribute sites

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Method:** five parallel research subagents across gaming, sci-fi/anime, music, space/science, and toys/collectibles. Each scored candidates against a shared rubric with the "device" axis weighted heaviest.
**Reference project:** the-pokemon-journey (30 years of Pokemon, ending in an interactive Pokédex + "pick your starter").
**Status:** research only. Nothing here is a build decision. See the Pokedex design contract for the reference pattern.

## TLDR

The differentiator for a topic to work in this format is a **Pokédex analog**: a structured catalog the page can *end* on (roster + stats + eras + a "pick your X" moment). Most candidates have one; a few don't.

**Recommended to develop into specs:**

- **Jungle / Drum & Bass** ("The Break Cabinet") — niche-but-loved, strongest device, safest audio licensing (CC0 chiptune).
- **Deep Sea** ("The Abyssal Catalog") — most unique scroll mechanic (you dive, the page darkens), clean 150th of the HMS Challenger.
- **Dragon Quest** ("The Monster Codex") — strongest franchise pick; the 40th is being celebrated live right now (Square Enix, May 27, 2026).

**Golden-anniversary mainstream options:** **Star Trek** (60th in 2026, the only "golden" milestone found) and **Mars** (Viking 50th this coming July 2026, NASA public-domain assets).

---

## What "fits this format" means

For a topic to map onto the-pokemon-journey, it needs all five:

1. **Fan love** — an identifiable, passionate community with nostalgia value.
2. **A clean anniversary arc** — a defensible "N years" milestone (state the years + the math). Prefer round numbers landing 2024-2027.
3. **An iconic visual identity** — a palette, signature shapes, and sprites recreatable offline.
4. **A Pokédex analog ("the device")** — the structured catalog that closes the page: a roster of collectibles/creatures/ships/cards with stats, "types," eras, and a "pick your X" finale. This is the most load-bearing axis.
5. **Chapter/route structure** — the story splits cleanly into eras/chapters ("routes").

Plus two practical axes this project is hard-bound on (all offline, no licensed media shipped): **signature sound** (ideally a CC0/chiptune-recreatable motif) and **asset availability** (public domain / CC0 / trivially recreatable; trademark posture must stay "recreate everything, use the name nominatively").

**Rubric used (1-5 each, device weighted heaviest):** fan love · anniversary · visual identity · device · chapters · sound · assets.

## Top niche-but-loved picks

### 1. Jungle / Drum & Bass — "The Break Cabinet" (music)

The cultiest, most fiercely-loved scene of all the candidates, and the safest on licensing. About 100 tracks cataloged by era (jungle -> golden D&B -> liquid/neuro/hard -> breakcore), each with a BPM "stat," key, and the break sample (Amen, Apache) as its "move." "Pick your BPM type" finale. The 30th of the 1996 golden-era peak.

- **Anniversary:** golden era peaked ~1994-96; 1996 -> 30th in 2026. Jungle entered the UK mainstream 1994; the "drum and bass" name landed 1995; tempos settled at 170-180 BPM in 1996.
- **Device:** "The Break Cabinet" — tracks with era / BPM / key / type (jungle, liquid, neuro, hard, breakcore) / the break sample. The closest real Pokédex analog in the music domain.
- **Chapters/routes:** Jungle (93-95) -> Golden D&B (95-96) -> The Splits (97-99: liquid/neuro/hard) -> 00s (grime adjacency, ~174) -> Now.
- **Sound/assets:** a 174 BPM amen-break loop + sub bass is trivially CC0-synth-able; no copyrighted melody needed. Safest audio position of every candidate.

### 2. Deep Sea — "The Abyssal Catalog" (science)

The most unique mechanic: you don't scroll down, you **dive**. The page darkens from photic turquoise to ink-black as it descends 0 m -> 10,916 m (Challenger Deep), with bioluminescent scenes that read like the glowing-creature beats of the reference site.

- **Anniversary:** the HMS Challenger expedition (1872-76, returned May 24, 1876) -> **150th in 2026**. The voyage that founded oceanography and cataloged 4,000+ new species is literally a pre-dated Pokédex. (Secondary: Trieste's manned Challenger Deep dive, Jan 23, 1960 -> 66th in 2026.)
- **Device:** "The Abyssal Catalog" — about 80 creatures with depth zone / size / bioluminescent (y/n) / rarity / first described (year + expedition). "Pick your creature."
- **Chapters/routes:** Sunlit (0-200 m) -> Twilight (200-1,000) -> Midnight (1,000-4,000) -> Abyssal (4,000-6,000) -> Hadal (6,000-10,916).
- **Sound/assets:** sonar ping + low hydrophone ambience (NOAA underwater soundings are openly licensed). Species photography is mixed licensing; the safe offline path is silhouettes + procedural glow.

### 3. Dragon Quest — "The Monster Codex" (gaming)

The "granddaddy of JRPGs," with a real Toriyama-designed monster bestiary (Slime, Gargoyle, Magma Lord, dragons), a genuine Pokédex rather than just characters.

- **Anniversary:** DQ I (Jan 1986, JP Famicom) -> **40th in 2026**, and it is alive right now: Square Enix ran a 40th-anniversary broadcast May 27, 2026. Natural reaction/news hook.
- **Device:** "The Dragon Quest Monster Codex" — monsters with stats / element / era.
- **Chapters/routes:** DQ I-XII, split by generation (8-bit -> 16-bit -> HD).
- **Sound/assets:** Kouchi Sugiyama's opening theme is legendary and chiptune-able. Square Enix licensing; recreate all art.

### 4. Galaxy Express 999 — "The 999 Manifest" (anime)

The clearest sci-fi cult gem: a chrome interstellar train, glowing red "1999" numerals, a small boy and an android woman on a death-ride.

- **Anniversary:** ran Sept 14, 1978 - Mar 28, 1981 (113 episodes) -> **48th in 2026**. Far less traveled than Gundam/Macross.
- **Device:** "The 999 Manifest" — passengers, the show's vampiric aliens, and visited planets, with a "fate" status column (alive / dead / ascended).
- **Sound/assets:** the G999 theme (Kunihiko Iwami), TMS copyright; recreate all art.

### 5. Golden Age of Arcade / Pong — "The 8-Bit Cabinet" (gaming era)

A clean half-century: Pong (1976) -> Space Invaders (1978) -> Pac-Man (1980) -> the golden age -> the '83 crash -> revival. The most natural companion to this project's existing chiptune DNA.

- **Anniversary:** Pong arcade 1976 -> **50th of video games / the arcade era in 2026**. (Magnavox Odyssey 1972 was the first home console.)
- **Device:** "The 8-Bit Arcade Cabinet" — a catalog of golden-age cabinets/games with year / maker / genre / coin-op / controls.
- **Sound/assets:** Pong beeps + Space Invaders' "ta-dum" are chiptune-able; public-knowledge 8-bit aesthetics carry the lowest licensing risk of any franchise candidate.

## Golden-anniversary mainstream options

### Star Trek — "The Species Compendium"

TOS premiered Sept 8, 1966 -> **60th in 2026**, the only "golden" milestone found across all domains.

- **Device:** a roster of alien species (Vulcans, Klingons, Ferengi, Romulans, Bajorans, Q, Borg) with type / homeworld / first-appearance era / signature tech. Maps almost 1:1 onto a Pokédex. (Alternative: a "Starship Registry.")
- **Chapters/routes:** TOS (66-69) -> films -> TNG (1987) -> DS9 (1994) -> VOY -> ENT (2001) -> streaming era (DIS/NOC/PIC).
- **Sound/assets:** the Star Trek theme (John Williams), Paramount copyright; recreate all sprites/imagery, use the name nominatively.

### Mars — "The Mars Dex"

Viking 1 landed July 20, 1976 -> **50th on July 20, 2026**, already being commemorated by the Mars Society and the Smithsonian Air & Space.

- **Device:** "The Mars Dex" — about 25 missions (Mariner 4 -> Viking -> Pathfinder/Sojourner -> the 2001/2003 rovers -> MRO -> Curiosity -> Perseverance/Ingenuity -> Tianwen-1/Zhurong) with type (flyby/orbiter/lander/rover), mass, landing site, instruments, years active, and a status rarity tier (active / crashed / retired). "Pick your rover."
- **Chapters/routes:** 1960s probes -> 1970s first landings -> 1990s renaissance -> 2000s rovers -> 2010s big science -> 2020s life hunt.
- **Sound/assets:** NASA/JPL imagery is public domain (credit, no-endorsement); ESA is CC BY-SA. The safest asset position of any candidate. No iconic licensed theme; a mission-control static -> chiptune "rover transmission" motif works.

## Device-first wildcards (weaker anniversary, stronger catalog)

- **Periodic Table — "The Periodic Dex."** 118 elements, the structurally cleanest Pokédex found (a closed canon with per-entry stats: atomic number, mass, state, discovery year + method, abundance, radioactivity). But 2026 is a 157th, not a round number; ship on "157 years" or target the 175th (2044).
- **Bioluminescence — "The Glow Atlas."** Black + neon-glow aesthetic is the closest visual match to the reference site's glowing-creature scenes (about 50 organisms with emission wavelength, organ, habitat zone). No defensible anniversary; it is a phenomenon, not a dated event.
- **Doctor Who — "The Monster Bestiary."** Arguably the single best device concept found (Daleks, Cybermen, Weeping Angels, Sontarans, Vashta Nerani with threat ratings). But 2026 is its 63rd year; the clean 60th already landed in 2023.

## Cross-cutting licensing posture

Every franchise candidate still carries trademark/copyright, so each one must use the same posture this site already does: **recreate all sprites/art offline, ship no licensed media, and use the brand name nominatively in a fan-tribute footer.**

- **Safest assets:** Mars/Space Race (NASA public domain, ESA CC), Golden Age/Pong (public-knowledge 8-bit), Jungle/DnB (CC0 chiptune breaks), Periodic Table (open scientific data).
- **Trademarked, recreate-everything:** Star Trek (Paramount), Dragon Quest (Square Enix), Galaxy Express 999 (TMS), Macross (Shochi/BigWest), Blade Runner (Disney), Blade Runner/Alien (Disney), TCGs (Wizards/Konami), toys (Hasbro/Mattel), Air Jordan (Nike, the most litigious of the group).
- **Audio risk ranking (lowest to highest):** Jungle/DnB, Hot Wheels, MTG, Air Jordan (original-only is natural) < G.I. Joe (catchphrase OK) < Yu-Gi-Oh (anime OP licensed) < Transformers G1 intro (most iconic, most licensed). Original recordings are copyrighted across the board; the safe pattern is CC0 chiptune/lo-fi/synth recreations of signature lines plus original text/data (BPM, key, sample source, era).

## Recommendation

Develop into specs, in order of fit to "niche but loved + strong device + safe assets":

1. **Jungle / Drum & Bass** — best device + safest audio + genuinely niche.
2. **Deep Sea** — most unique scroll mechanic + clean 150th.
3. **Dragon Quest** — the strongest franchise pick, being celebrated live in 2026.

If a bigger-name golden anniversary is preferred, **Star Trek (60th)** and **Mars (Viking 50th)** are the two that land in 2026.
